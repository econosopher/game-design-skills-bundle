# Flow Audit

Adapted from Stanislav Stankovic's design lens.

Map the experience over time: goals, challenge, required skills, information, feedback, failure costs and opportunities to learn. Identify where these demands change and which players encounter them. A single difficulty rating hides differences in prior skill and preferences.

Treat boredom, absorption, frustration and anxiety as experiences to investigate with appropriate reports or behavioral evidence, not automatic names for points on a challenge-versus-skill chart. Low challenge may be desired relaxation; high challenge may be desired competition. Do not equate easy success with disengagement or repeated failure with churn.

For each candidate breakpoint, compare the required action with actual ability, feedback and learning opportunity. Inspect latency, ambiguity and incentive problems before attributing the outcome to a flow mismatch. Recommend a change, explain its costs and state what observation would disconfirm the mechanism. Report pacing and retention separately.
