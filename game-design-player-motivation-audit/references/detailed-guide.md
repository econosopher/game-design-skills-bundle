# Player Motivation Audit

Adapted from Stanislav Stankovic's named design lens. Unsupported outcome claims and prescriptive psychological judgments have been removed.

## Vocabulary and evidence

The upstream SDT-inspired spectrum names amotivation, external regulation, introjected regulation, identified regulation, integrated regulation and intrinsic motivation. In plain terms these distinguish no intention to act, external outcomes, self-evaluation pressure, personally valued outcomes, identity-aligned goals and enjoyment of the activity itself. These are candidate explanations, not a ranking of player worth or automatic predictors of revenue and retention.

Start with the target action and its net benefits: rewards, time, effort, alternatives, social consequences and information. Different reasons can generate the same action. Use player reports to investigate experience and behavior to test responses to changed choices; neither uniquely identifies the other. Do not conclude that extrinsic motivation is unsustainable or that intrinsic rewards are insufficient for casual players.

When relevant, inspect perceived choice, competence and relationships. A published [four-study SDT paper](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf) reports relationships with enjoyment and preferences in studied settings. It does not calibrate a universal commercial effect or validate this bundle's moral judgments. Distinguish observational evidence from causal identification and test transportability to the current game.

A useful output states observed action, possible reason, alternative explanation, relevant evidence and a discriminating test. Avoid a mandatory full spectrum audit for a narrow question.
