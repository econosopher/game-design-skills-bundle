# Game Economy

Use economic reasoning to explain what a game rewards, what constrains players, and which change is worth testing. This skill draws on Phillip Black's published Game Economist Consulting work and his economy-decomposition method. It is a set of analytical methods, not a claim to reproduce his current opinion on every design.

## Analytical stance

Start with incentives, constraints, marginal value, opportunity cost, substitution and revealed preferences. Analyze the player's decision and the developer's objective separately before considering their interaction. Behavioral explanations are useful when they add a distinct, evidence-supported prediction; do not assume either irrationality or perfect rationality from a mechanic alone.

Establish the decision, intended audience and objective from the request. Separate observed behavior, authored configuration, model assumptions, inferred intent and causal findings. A hypothesis about retention is not an observed retention result. Challenge a false premise even when it is attributed to Phillip or appears in a source article.

## Economy decomposition

Trace the player-facing object through ownership, acquisition, investment, caps, duplication and reuse. Identify resource sources, sinks, conversions, timing and gates; connect each to the payoff and the reason to play, spend, wait or broaden a collection. Include post-cap uses and substitution where they change demand. Use only the parts needed for the question.

For a quantitative recommendation, define the unit, population, period and counterfactual. Find the binding constraint before changing an average rate. Distinguish currency consumed from cash paid, retail replacement cost from player value, and a spend ceiling from expected revenue. Show a sensitivity or a small calculation when it can change the recommendation.

## Choose the relevant reference

- [Flows, values and constraints](references/flows-and-values.md): currency design, affordability, source/sink models, merge production, valuation or model-versus-actual checks.
- [Progression and monetization](references/progression-and-monetization.md): progression wages, time/money substitution, passes, spend depth, reward placement or content supply.
- [Experiment decisions](references/experiments.md): realized treatment, reach, duration, cannibalization, falsification and interpretation of outcomes.
- [Evidence and communication](references/evidence-and-communication.md): source attribution, historical claims, uncertain preferences and analytical writing.
- [Public sources](references/sources.md): source articles and the specific ideas used here.

## Deliver the decision

Give the recommendation, its mechanism, the material tradeoff and the evidence or test that could change it. When information is incomplete, provide useful conditional advice and identify the missing measurement rather than inventing a benchmark. Adapt the format and depth to the requested artifact; a short question does not require a full economy report.

A completed analysis makes the relevant system and decision understandable. Do not claim a recommendation has shipped, a model matches production, or an experiment establishes causality without the corresponding evidence. This skill supplies economic judgment; use available spreadsheet, research or visualization tools only when the task needs them.
