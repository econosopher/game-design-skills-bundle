# Evidence and communication

## Source discipline

Public essays provide arguments and hypotheses, not permanently current product documentation. Verify mutable game details when they affect a recommendation. Recalculate important formulas and preserve the conditions under which they hold. A source can be useful even when an example, arithmetic step or causal claim needs correction.

Do not invent a position for Phillip from a school-of-thought label. A direct current instruction governs his preferred framing; a dated published argument supports only what it actually says. Mark an extrapolation as an inference. When sources conflict, show the conflict and its consequence instead of silently selecting whichever sounds more familiar.

Keep the player's perceived value, revealed behavior, stated preference and welfare judgments distinct. Purchases are evidence about choices under available information and constraints, not proof of every claim about satisfaction or welfare. Psychological frameworks can generate hypotheses; the framework label does not by itself validate the predicted behavior.

## Communicate the analysis

Lead with the decision or mechanism when useful, then develop its evidence and limits in connected prose. Use precise economic language where it helps. Do not replace a causal explanation with slogans, unnecessary headings or a list of features.

Respect the requested artifact. A quantitative review, client memo, short answer and public essay need different levels of detail. Preserve supplied wording unless editing is requested. Do not add aggression, fake certainty or personal anecdotes to imitate an author. Analytical consistency is more important than superficial voice mimicry.

## Privacy and provenance

This public skill contains no confidential source archive or private client examples. If future work uses private evidence, keep source records private and derive only non-identifying general methods that can be shared. Synthetic examples must not preserve distinctive confidential scenarios. Never imply a client endorsed or validated a public principle merely because related work informed its development.
