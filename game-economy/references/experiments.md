# Experiment decisions

## Assignment is not realized treatment

State the changed configuration, the economic mechanism it is intended to alter, and the player experience that actually changed. More moves, a larger reward or an extra mode is an assignment; changed success rate, earned progression or incremental play is a realized treatment.

Use treatment strength, player reach and duration as a scoping heuristic. Their product is not a universal causal-effect estimator. Investigate eligibility, exposure, uptake and mechanism metrics before interpreting a flat top-line result as rejection of the underlying idea.

Define what evidence would falsify the decision-relevant hypothesis and what action follows. Set analysis populations and guardrails before inspecting outcomes where possible. Weak exposure, implementation failure, low precision and a true null have different implications.

## Counterfactuals and substitution

A feature can gain usage by taking it from another feature. Measure adoption and gross activity, then estimate displaced activity and net change relative to a credible counterfactual. The same principle applies across games in a portfolio and across purchase offers. Avoid summing gains across overlapping interventions as if they were independent.

Match observation windows to the mechanism. A temporary lift need not persist, and a long-lived economy change may take time to affect holdings and purchasing. A before/after comparison without controls is not equivalent to randomized evidence. Account for attrition and heterogeneous exposure when interpreting cohort averages.

## Useful test framing

For the decision at hand, identify:

- What changes, for whom and for how long.
- The intermediate behavior or economic flow that should move.
- The relevant outcome and counterfactual, with cost and displacement where applicable.
- What result would justify proceeding, revising or stopping.

Do not demand a new experiment when existing evidence already answers the question, or when the user only asked for an explanation.

Synthetic example: an added mode receives 20 matches per exposed player, while existing-mode play falls by 18 relative to control. Its gross adoption is 20 matches; the measured net addition is 2. That alone says nothing about profitability without values and costs for each activity.
