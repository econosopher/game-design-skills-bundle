# Game Design Unknown Unknowns Prototyping

Map design uncertainty and choose inexpensive learning prototypes before committing an underdefined concept to production.

## Design lens

**Preproduction handles known unknowns. Prototyping explores unknown unknowns.**

That distinction matters.
- **Known unknowns** are questions the team already knows to ask.
- **Unknown unknowns** are hidden design problems, emergent opportunities, and unexpected interactions that only become visible through testing.

Therefore:
- do not prototype to mimic production
- prototype to expose uncertainty
- prototype to discover what the game might actually be

## Result

a prototyping plan with these outputs:

- **Concept framing** - what the team thinks the idea is, and where it is still foggy
- **Uncertainty map** - what is known, suspected, and unexplored
- **Prototype questions** - what must be learned through making and testing
- **Prototype sequence** - what order to test things in, and how each test informs the next
- **Stop criteria** - when to stop exploring and move toward preproduction or production framing
- **Decision record** - what was learned, what died, and what stronger direction emerged
