# Zeigarnik Effect Audit

Adapted from Stanislav Stankovic's named design lens. Unsupported outcome claims and prescriptive psychological judgments have been removed.

## Separate recall from return

The upstream unfinished-task lens conflated memory advantage, resumption and product retention. Remove the rule that unfinished things necessarily stick in the mind and the classification of open loops as healthy or toxic.

[Research on interruption, recall and resumption](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05000-w) distinguishes the Zeigarnik memory claim from the Ovsiankina resumption tendency. Its 2025 meta-analysis challenges a general memory advantage while finding a resumption tendency. Neither establishes a retention lift for a quest log or pass.

Map each unfinished item: remaining reward, progress, deadline, expected effort, reminders, visibility and the player's alternatives. Test whether apparent return behavior is explained by remaining rewards, sunk-cost reasoning, salience, reminders or task completion preferences. Do not assume the name of an effect proves its mechanism.

Measure recall, resumption, total play and reported experience separately. A reminder experiment can change salience without identifying a memory mechanism. Claims about guilt, anxiety, coercion or enjoyment require their own evidence and definitions.

Recommend simplifying, staging, resolving or retaining open tasks only in relation to the requested outcome and tested mechanism. A large quest log may be useful optional content rather than a burden.
